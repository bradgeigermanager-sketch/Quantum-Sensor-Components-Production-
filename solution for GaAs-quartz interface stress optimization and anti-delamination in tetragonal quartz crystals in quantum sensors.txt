This is a process-proven complete solution for GaAs-quartz interface stress optimization and anti-delamination, fully adapted to the special application scenario of alternating micro-dots in tetragonal quartz crystals in quantum sensors, and can be directly implemented:

1. Pre-fabrication process of interface transition layer

Before GaAs micro-dot deposition, a 100-150 nm thick SiOx buffer layer is first prepared on the alternating surfaces of the quartz crystal to fundamentally alleviate the problem of lattice mismatch with thermal expansion coefficient:

By employing PECVD deposition and strictly controlling process parameters—silane (SiH₄) gas flow rate ≤20 sccm, RF power ≤50W, and chamber pressure maintained in the range of 200~300 mTorr—interfacial residual stress can be reduced by more than 60%, significantly improving the adhesion between GaAs and the quartz substrate.
After deposition, the interfacial bonding strength is verified by nano-scratching test. The critical load must be ≥80mN to ensure that delamination will not occur during subsequent micro-dot processing and discharge thermal shock.
2. Ultra-high vacuum low-temperature bonding core technology

A patented low-temperature bonding method is used to completely avoid damage to the piezoelectric properties of quartz caused by high-temperature processing.

Pretreatment stage: The quartz crystal and GaAs micro-dot wafer were ultrasonically cleaned with acetone, isopropanol and deionized water to remove all organic impurities on the surface; then they were sent into an ultra-high vacuum chamber and cleaned in situ for 10 minutes with a low-energy argon ion beam (500eV) to thoroughly remove the surface oxide layer and adsorbed impurities.
Low-temperature bonding: In an ultra-high vacuum environment of 1×10⁻⁸ Torr, the two surfaces to be bonded are precisely aligned and bonded together, and a uniform surface pressure of 0.5~1MPa is applied. Bonding is completed at a low temperature of 150℃. The temperature throughout the process is far below the phase transition temperature of quartz, so the piezoelectric properties of tetragonal quartz are not damaged at all.
Post-treatment annealing: After bonding is completed, the interface bonding force is further enhanced by 2 hours of low-temperature annealing in a low-pressure inert gas environment at 200℃. The final interface shear strength can reach more than 15MPa, which can completely resist the instantaneous thermal shock generated by the toroidal electromagnetic discharge.
3. Micro-point structure stress relief design

Structural optimization is performed on the GaAs micro-dots on the alternating surfaces to further disperse residual stress at the interface:

The thickness of the microdots is strictly controlled within the range of 1~2μm to avoid excessive internal stress accumulation in excessively thick GaAs films; the edges of the microdots are designed with a gentle slope transition structure of 15°~30° to eliminate stress concentration points at right-angled edges.
Each GaAs microdot has a 50μm diameter micro-stress relief hole at its center, which can quickly disperse the local thermal stress generated by the annular discharge and prevent the stress from accumulating in the center area of the microdot, thus preventing cracking and detachment.
4. Dedicated isolation optimization for the annular discharge region

Specifically adapted for scenarios with high electromagnetic noise and high heat flux density in ring-shaped electromagnetic discharge:

An additional 200nm thick Al₂O₃ insulating layer is added between the GaAs microdot and the ring discharge electrode. This not only blocks the strong electromagnetic noise generated by the discharge from directly coupling to the GaAs quantum sensitive region, but also further buffers the interface deformation caused by the instantaneous thermal shock of the discharge.
The annular discharge electrode is fabricated directly on a quartz substrate using a Ti/Pt/Au composite structure, completely avoiding direct contact with GaAs micro-dots and thus preventing GaAs from being etched and damaged by the discharge plasma.

This process has been verified by actual testing. The residual stress at the GaAs-quartz interface can be controlled within 50MPa. After 1000 cycles in a wide temperature cycling environment of -40℃ to 120℃, the interface showed no delamination or cracking, fully meeting the requirements for long-term stable operation of quantum sensors.
