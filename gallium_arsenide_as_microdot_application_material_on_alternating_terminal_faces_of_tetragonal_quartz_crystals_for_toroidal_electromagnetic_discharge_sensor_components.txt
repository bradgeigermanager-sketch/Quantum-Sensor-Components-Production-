Gallium arsenide (GaAs) is ‌not universally ideal‌ for this specific microdot application on alternating faces of tetragonal quartz crystals in quantum sensors, especially for the toroidal electromagnetic discharge sections — it delivers strong performance in some critical dimensions but has notable hard limitations that prevent it from being a perfect fit for this exact use case.

✅ Key advantages that make GaAs a strong candidate
High electrothermal conversion efficiency‌: GaAs has far higher thermal resistivity than silicon, enabling it to operate stably at elevated temperatures while maintaining excellent electrothermal conversion performance, which is well-suited for the high-field, energy-dense environment around toroidal discharge structures .
Native plasma oscillation properties‌: Thin-film high-purity GaAs layers (1–2 μm) can generate stable electron-hole plasma under high electric fields (>200 kV/cm), producing coherent microwave-band plasma oscillations whose amplitude and frequency are highly sensitive to external magnetic fields. This characteristic directly matches the electromagnetic field sensing requirements of quantum sensor discharge components .
Quantum-grade material compatibility‌: GaAs quantum dots are already a mature platform for generating near-unity indistinguishability single photons, with proven performance in quantum information, quantum sensing and hybrid quantum systems. It can be seamlessly integrated with strain-tunable piezoelectric substrates (the same material family as tetragonal quartz) to achieve precise, reversible tuning of quantum emission properties .
MEMS process maturity ‌: GaAs micromachining technology is highly developed, and it has been widely used to fabricate RF/microwave power sensors, infrared bolometers and other microscale sensing devices, which can support the high-precision microdot patterning required for this application.
❌ Critical limitations for this specific quartz-based toroidal discharge scenario
Lattice and thermal mismatch with quartz ‌: GaAs and tetragonal quartz have very different thermal expansion coefficients and lattice structures. Directly depositing GaAs microdots on alternating quartz faces will introduce large residual stress, which degrades the piezoelectric resonance performance of quartz and may cause microdot delamination under the thermal shock generated by toroidal electromagnetic discharge.
Not optimized for quartz's alternating-face electrode configuration ‌: Most existing GaAs-quantum piezoelectric integration schemes use dedicated piezoelectric actuator substrates such as PMN-PT, rather than bare tetragonal quartz. There is no mature verified process for patterning GaAs microdots on the alternating orthogonal faces of bulk quartz crystals.
Charge noise risk ‌: Even optimized GaAs quantum dot structures still have inherent charge noise that causes spectral jitter. In the high electromagnetic noise environment around toroidal discharge, this effect will be amplified, increasing the decoherence risk of quantum states.
Final conclusion

GaAs is an excellent functional material for the quantum sensing part of the device, but it is not the ideal direct microdot material for the toroidal electromagnetic discharge sections on alternating quartz faces. The recommended optimized scheme is to use GaAs for the quantum-sensitive microdot region, and use a more mature metal-dielectric composite electrode material for the toroidal discharge contact points, to avoid the interface mismatch problem between GaAs and quartz.
